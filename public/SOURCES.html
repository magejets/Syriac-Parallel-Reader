Texts & sources

This prototype presents all 50 chapters of Genesis. It is an independent reader and has no affiliation with Parallel Plus, Sefaria, Open Scriptures or ETCBC.

Hebrew

The Masoretic text is the Westminster Leningrad Codex from the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible Project. The project states that the WLC text is public domain; its lemma and morphology annotations are CC BY 4.0. Credit: Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible Project. Prefix separators have been removed for reading; verse punctuation is retained. The written form (ketiv) is displayed, with recorded qere available in word study. Cantillation can be hidden without hiding vowels.

Hebrew lexicon

Headwords, glosses, lexical roots, transliterations and BDB entries derive from Open Scriptures Hebrew Lexicon, licensed CC BY 4.0. The underlying 1906 BDB is public domain. This is their abridged transcription, not a new full BDB edition. XML markup has been converted to plain text; formatting and page references may be reduced. Root groups follow supplied lexical etymology, not guessed consonant matches. Prefix codes without lexical entries are explicitly marked.

Syriac

The consonantal Genesis text and annotations come from ETCBC / CACCHT Syriac Corpus v0.9 (compiler Martijn Naaijer; collaboration of the Eep Talstra Centre for Bible and Computer and CACCHT). Its Peshitta text is based on Vetus Testamentum Syriace, prepared by the Peshitta Institute Amsterdam, formerly Leiden. The critical apparatus is not included. The upstream edition is not a public-domain transcription.

Use this bundled Syriac text only noncommercially. The separate ETCBC Peshitta data notice specifies CC BY-NC for the plain text and distinguishes it from MIT conversion software. The annotated corpus revision has an MIT repository LICENSE but does not give a separate explicit text license in its README. That discrepancy is not treated here as permission to relicense edition-derived text under MIT. Before commercial use, or treating this annotated release as unrestricted open data, obtain clarification from ETCBC and the edition rights holders. The noncommercial restriction is a conservative upstream constraint, not a claim that the annotated repository explicitly specifies a particular CC version.

The upstream Peshitta citation is Willem Th. van Peursen, Geert Jan Veldman, Constantijn Sikkel, Hannes Vlaardingerbroek and Dirk Roorda, ETCBC/peshitta v0.3 (2018), doi:10.5281/zenodo.1464757. For the annotated corpus see its project DOI and the pinned v0.9 commit in the revision record. Contributor credits are preserved in the source README included with the project.

Attached morphemes have been joined using the source's trailer feature, preserving Syriac shaping. Lexemes remain in ETCBC transcription. Glosses are lexical aids, not translations of each occurrence. Word study includes an unvocalized consonant transliteration based on the base-letter repertoire in the Unicode Syriac chart. It does not infer vowels or pronunciation; unknown letters remain visible. No Syriac vowels, roots, cognate links or new word alignments have been invented.

Fonts

Self-hosted Noto Sans Syriac (Estrangela) and Noto Serif Hebrew are distributed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Their copyright and reserved-name notices are preserved in Syriac OFL and Hebrew OFL. All fonts work offline. The writing style is a font choice, not a separate Unicode alphabet.

Optional Sefaria lookup

The reader contacts Sefaria's lexicon API only when you choose the online lookup button. This sends the Hebrew query and verse reference to Sefaria. It displays matching headwords and dictionary names with links, without saving definitions. Static BDB and concordance remain independent of the API, but require a connection; only scripture and fonts are saved for offline reading.

Sefaria licenses vary by resource and version; see its copyright and data-use guidance. A future full-entry provider must check resource-specific licenses, sanitize definitions, and handle ambiguity, network failures and offline fallbacks. An API match does not guarantee the correct sense for this verse.

Alignment & reproducibility

All 1,533 Genesis references match in the export. Pairs are based on matching chapter/verse labels and have not undergone a complete philological alignment audit. Word positions are never used to infer translation equivalents. Occurrences cover Genesis only, and Hebrew counts follow the displayed ketiv. Multi-component root groups may include more than one token within a displayed word.
